About Me

I’m a CS student who likes building products that feel fast, clear, and respectful of the user. I care a lot about accessibility because it’s where “good engineering” and “good ethics” overlap. If someone can’t use what we build, it doesn’t matter how clever it is.

Outside of class, I’m into music and software. I enjoy making tools that reduce friction so people can focus on what they’re actually trying to do. I also like learning by shipping small things quickly and improving them.

My Accessibility Interests

What I’m most interested in

  • Semantic HTML and keyboard-friendly UI patterns
  • Readable layouts and strong information hierarchy
  • Forms that are easy to complete and hard to mess up

How I try to apply it

I’m trying to get better at building interfaces that don’t rely on “guessing.” That means clear labels, consistent navigation, and feedback that helps users recover from mistakes. I also try to avoid building features that only work for mouse users.

One thing I’ve learned: accessible design often makes the experience better for everyone, not just people using assistive technology.

Quick accessibility checklist I like

A simple checklist I use when reviewing a page.
Area What I check
Keyboard Can I reach everything without a mouse?
Headings Do headings form a clear outline (H1 → H2 → H3)?
Images Do meaningful images have helpful alt text?
